Summer 2026 Cohort · Completed

David Mykel Taylor
Scholars

Eight Morehouse scholars. Ten weeks. A summer spent building real infrastructure, learning alongside industry mentors, and discovering where their skills could take them.

From June 29 through September 4, 2026, the David Mykel Taylor Scholars turned classroom knowledge into hands-on experience with servers, Linux, networking, and the people behind Atlanta’s data centers.

SUMMER 2026 · JUNE 29–SEPTEMBER 4

A Summer Worth Remembering

The memories are in the work we did together: connecting real equipment, asking better questions, and meeting professionals who made a future in infrastructure feel within reach.

July 13, 2026 · Learning with Splunk

Learning to See the Whole System

With Splunk’s Damian Herrick, scholars explored logs, dashboards, and AI-assisted investigation. Questions about a Python/FastAPI dashboard and Kubernetes monitoring connected the session to the systems students were building.

Summer outcomes

Experience That Opened a Door

The paid, ten-week program paired technical work with employer visits, résumé reviews, and interview practice. The cohort produced an Amazon data center operations offer—a concrete next step from a summer of learning.

Thank you to QTS and AARI’s wider partner and mentor community for helping make this summer possible. See the summer cohort results.

In Memory

May 5, 2001 - Forever 23

David Mykel Taylor

Born in Atlanta on May 5, 2001, David was a Georgia State University student, musician, volunteer, and a familiar presence at the GSU, Morehouse, and Spelman bookstores. He showed up for his community with creativity, confidence, and determination.

The David Mykel Taylor Scholars program honors that spirit: meeting adversity with purpose, continuing to build, and bringing others along. His legacy lives in the opportunities students create for themselves and the next generation.

THE FELLOWSHIP

Learn It. Build It. Pass It On.

AARI connects college scholars with hands-on infrastructure experience and opportunities to mentor younger learners. The work combines technical practice, industry relationships, and service.

Build real systems

Practice Linux, networking, server operations, and robotics. Document the work and learn to explain how the pieces fit together.

Learn with professionals

Connect lab work to careers through industry visits, technical conversations, and feedback from experienced mentors.

Teach the next generation

Help younger students explore STEM and robotics through hands-on activities. Build leadership by helping someone else learn.
